# OutputMemory guard: producer invariant and compatibility

The condition is `!word100[1] || word78[1:0] == 0`. Word 100 bit 1 is `mo_wr`; word 78 holds the delay-zero slot/stage counter. OutputGenerator uses delay 8, and both 8 and the modulo-72 counter wrap preserve stage modulo 4.

The proof is over control transitions, not over musical samples. At reset, the counter is 71 and `mo_wr=0`. On an enabled unheld stage-3 edge, OutputGenerator sets `mo_wr=1` while SlotCounter advances to stage 0. An enabled stage-0 edge clears `mo_wr` and advances to stage 1. Stages 1 and 2 preserve the flag, which must already be zero. A raw edge without enable, or any held edge, preserves both fields. Exhaustive induction over the 72 counter positions, invariant-consistent flags, enable and hold gives 360 valid transitions. Rhythm, keys, instruments and synthesis data do not enter these control assignments. Asynchronous reset restores the base case; sequential memory initialization does not change the flag/counter rule.

CAP_READ/CAP_STORE read all 104 words while FM hold is asserted. Therefore the non-simultaneous reads of words 78 and 100 observe the same frozen control epoch. LOAD may temporarily install word 78 before word 100, but consumers and operational writes remain held until the entire payload is applied and refilled. The new guard validates before any FM apply. A complete accepted LOAD re-establishes the invariant. Legacy fallback installs count 71 and clears word 100, so it also establishes the invariant without changing its behavior.

`audit_producers.js` examined 110 full-history producer/control changes reachable from all local refs, including merges, and all current worktrees. Four version-17 producer commits were found: `7fcd5e12`, `854761c3`, `1b2a52d8`, and `98737615`. Their OutputGenerator, SlotCounter and snapshot transport form one identical normalized source group. Branches inheriting those files are covered by the same group. All current version-17 worktrees preserve those source controls. The minimal experiment's different descriptor is not a version-17 producer. There was no remote fetch, so this is a local-known-producer audit, not certification of unknown external writers.

The enclosing SAVE FSMs were also inspected. Functional v19 freezes at the accepted enabled edge, holding T+1; the committed current-master integration freezes one ARM raw edge later. Both stop the flag and counter together before the sequential private capture. This distinction matters: v19 can save pending write data that has not reached RAM. The guard deliberately does not compare pending data with RAM or require pending flags to be zero.

The baseline capture-boundary campaign produces 6,480 images at all 72 phases and 15 quiescent offsets across six musical scenarios. The additional v19 early-freeze campaign produces 432 images across all 72 phases and the same six scenarios. Of those early images, 91 contain pending OutputMemory data different from RAM, and 54 contain pending RegisterMemory data different from RAM. They satisfy the new guard and pass the actual production validator, preserving this legitimate pending-write behavior.

Collision independence follows from the accepted condition. With `mo_wr=0`, initial refilled reads are deterministic and there is no resumed output write before a normal stage-3 transition. With `mo_wr=1` at stage 0, repeated raw writes can produce arbitrary registered read results, but stage 0 consumes neither port and clears the flag. A write-free read is available before the stage-2 mixer and stage-3 feedback consumers. This argument does not require other pipeline fields to be coherent; their scalar address bounds and the derived delayed counter are sufficient for this particular collision property. Snapshot writes and initialization can collide, but their sequencing keeps these results away from active consumers. The underlying MLAB read-during-write contract is still DONT_CARE.

No legitimate image from the audited producers, starting from reset, supported legacy initialization or a coherent full-state LOAD, is rejected. This is nevertheless an intentional acceptance tightening: previously accepted range-legal incoherent payloads with `mo_wr=1` outside stage 0 are now rejected. An old invalid LOAD followed by a rapid resave could preserve such incoherence; unknown third-party writers are also not certified. No universal compatibility claim is made for those cases. The guard is not a comprehensive musical-coherence validator.

RegisterMemory and FeedbackMemory remain logic. The guard does not attempt to solve their distinct collision cases or impose speculative restrictions on their saved fields.
